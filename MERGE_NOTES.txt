Review closely: eager dispatch
- Main added eager grants after FCW moved local limiter state out of
  rateLimitManager. Keeping main's grantTokens would create independent counters
  for eager and regular dispatch and allow the combined rate to exceed the limit.
- GrantEagerDispatch now calls fcManager.grantEagerTokens, then
  fc.Readiness.GrantLocalTokens. Whole-queue and fairness-key capacity are checked
  and consumed together under the namespace readiness shard lock. This shares
  limiter identities, counters, clipping, waiter notifications, and GC pinning
  with regular dispatch. Partial grants and main's priority ordering are retained.
- Per your instruction, any configured queue concurrency limit denies eager
  grants, without consuming rate tokens. Supporting concurrency grants later
  requires carrying limiter refs through eager starts and releasing them.
- Main's rollout gate remains: system.enableActivityEagerDispatchCheck defaults
  to false. Enable it for the namespace to enforce FC on eager workflow activities
  and standalone activities. With it disabled, History bypasses Matching grants.
- Eager workflow starts (service/history/api/startworkflow) are a separate path:
  they do not call the activity-grant API and remain outside FC enforcement.
  Disable system.enableEagerWorkflowStart where workflow queue concurrency limits
  must be enforced until that path is integrated. This rewrite covers the new
  Matching GrantEagerDispatch path and its activity callers.
- Concurrency denials currently count in the existing eager "rate_limit" metric
  outcome. There is no separate concurrency outcome in the existing RPC path.
- Added coverage for shared eager/regular counters in both directions, initial
  and partial grants, fairness-key isolation, preservation of other keys' capacity,
  concurrent grants, and concurrency rejection without token consumption.
